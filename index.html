Improving Individual Student Performance in Jasmine Ramey's Classroom

What the data shows

Of the 19 students with submitted work, 17 meet or exceed proficiency (raw score 5-8), while 2 students score in the developing range (4) and none show no evidence of learning.

Insights and implications

  • Capitalization and spelling deficiencies are systemic mechanics issues affecting the class broadly. Use daily whole-class mini-lessons and peer-review routines rather than relying only on one-to-one intervention.
  • Progression weakness reflects a need for stronger modeling of organizational structures and transitions. Students need support sequencing and connecting ideas logically.
  • Tolbert, Chase and Patel, Avery need scaffolded multi-domain support in organization and language conventions, including sentence-building and outlining practice before full essays.
  • The majority of the class meets or exceeds proficiency, so focused intervention for the two developing students and whole-class mechanics practice can move more students to Advanced performance.

Recommended actions

  • High: Run 5-minute daily capitalization and spelling mini-lessons using recent student writing samples, followed by peer-editing checklists.
  • High: Teach explicit organization scaffolds: graphic organizers, transition sentence stems, shared writing, then independent practice.
  • High: Give Tolbert, Chase and Patel, Avery guided sentence-combining, outlining, and small-group drafting check-ins.
  • Medium: Use strong performers as peer models during writing conferences to share organization and mechanics habits.

Data limitations

Skill ratings are sub-skill observations. A student can have multiple ratings across domains, so they do not represent separate students. This analysis covers one assessment and should be validated with additional evidence over time.
