Improving Individual Student Performance in Jasmine Ramey's Classroom

What the data shows

Of the 19 students with submitted work, 17 meet or exceed proficiency (raw score 5–8), while 2 students score in the developing range (4) and none show no evidence of learning.

Insights and implications

  • Capitalization and Spelling deficiencies are systemic mechanics issues affecting the class broadly, not isolated to one or two students. These require daily, whole-class mini-lessons and peer review routines rather than one-on-one intervention. (source)
  • Progression weakness (6 Weak ratings) reflects insufficient modeling of organizational structures and transitions. Students understand individual ideas but struggle to sequence and connect them logically—a teaching strategy gap, not a student capability gap. (source)
  • Tolbert, Chase and Patel, Avery require scaffolded, multi-domain support. Both show weakness in Purpose & Organization (Progression, Transitions) and Language conventions (Capitalization, Spelling), suggesting they need foundational sentence-building and outlining practice before tackling full essays. (source)
  • The majority of the class (17 of 19 students) meets or exceeds proficiency, indicating strong overall instruction. Targeted interventions for the two developing students and class-wide mechanics practice will move most students to Advanced performance. (source)

Recommended actions

  • High: Implement 5-minute daily capitalization and spelling mini-lessons using student writing samples. Focus on rules (proper nouns, sentence starts) and common misspellings from recent submissions. Pair with peer-editing checklists so students self-correct before submission. (source)
  • High: Teach explicit organizational scaffolds: graphic organizers for Progression (beginning–middle–end or claim–evidence–analysis), sentence stems for Transitions ('First, I will explain…' 'This shows that…'). Model with shared writing before independent practice. (source)
  • High: For Tolbert, Chase and Patel, Avery: Begin with sentence-combining and outlining exercises before full essays. Use guided practice with sentence frames and pre-written topic sentences. Provide one-on-one or small-group check-ins during drafting to catch capitalization and spelling errors early. (source)
  • Medium: Celebrate and reinforce strong performers (Martinez, Nasir; Flores, Olivia; Gibson, Jaxon; Ellison, Riley) as peer models. Have them share their organizational strategies and mechanics habits with the class during writing conferences. (source)

Data limitations

Skill ratings reflect observations at the sub-skill level, not individual student counts. A student may have multiple ratings across domains; the data does not isolate which specific students drive each gap, only that the gap exists across the cohort.

The analysis covers one assessment (Mar-9 GA Extended Writing Task - Athletic Ability). Trends and patterns may differ across other writing types or assessments; recommendations should be validated with additional evidence over time.
